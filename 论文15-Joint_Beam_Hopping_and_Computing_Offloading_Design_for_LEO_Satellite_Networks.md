Nonetheless, existing works   on   computing   offloading   primarily   focus   on   satellite systems with fixed beams, whose static beam illumination strategy limits the flexibility of resource allocation. Considering the spatial and temporal distribution of tasks, this paper jointly investigates the   beam   hopping   and   task   offloading   for   low   earth   orbit (LEO) multi-satellite multi-beam systems. With the objective of minimizing the maximum latency, the joint beam hopping and computing offloading optimization problem is formulated as a mixed integer programming problem. To address the original optimization problem, it is decomposed into two sub-problems: beam hopping time plan (BHTP) design, and joint computing offloading with computation resource allocation. Specifically, with a given BHTP, the joint computing offloading and computation resource allocation sub-problem is first analyzed and solved. Sub- sequently, by iteratively solving both sub-problems, the solutions for the original problem can be obtained. The effectiveness of the proposed joint beam hopping and computing offloading (JBHCO- MB) algorithm is validated through simulation, showing clear advantages over referred algorithms. In   MEC enhanced LEO satellite networks, users can not only process data locally but also offload their tasks to LEO satellites [6]. Thus, it is particularly beneficial when ground users face in- tensive processing demands that exceed their local computing capabilities, making satellite mobile edge computing (SMEC) a promising solution [7]. Although SMEC has been studied in many existing works, it is still an open topic to design effective strategies for joint computing offloading and multi-dimensional resources allocation. The specific demands and available communication resources will impact the design of computing offloading strategies, which in turn affects the amount of offloaded tasks, and ultimately influences the design of computation resource allocation strategy. As a result, designing efficient strategies for multi-dimensional resource allocation that meet the specific needs of different users remains a major challenge [8]. In addition, the diverse channel conditions across system nodes must be considered, which further increases the complexity of resource allocation [9], [10]. Currently, extensive works on satellite edge computing have primarily focused on multi-beam satellites with fixed beam illumination [11]. However, the tasks distribution in LEO networks is uneven [12], [13]. Users in different geographical positions have different requirements, and the requirements of the same user may change at different times. The fixed beam illumination approach lacks flexibility, which cannot match the users’ requirements dynamically, and thus reduces the utilization efficiency of system resources. By exploiting the spatial and temporal flexibility of beams, the beam hopping (BH) technology can effectively address the limitations of fixed beam illumination. BH technology enables a satellite to dynamically illuminate different geographic areas at different time slots according to demand and allows each beam to reuse the entire system spectrum, thereby improving the utilization of limited spectral resources and enhancing sys- tem flexibility [14], [15]. Nowadays, the rapid growth of users’ real-time traffic demands has imposed higher requirements on performance optimization in LEO satellite systems, and extensive works have been conducted on the application of BH for LEO satellite systems, including interference mitigation, beam   hopping   time   plan   (BHTP)   design,   radio   resources management, etc. In this paper, we leverage the advantages of BH and integrate it with SMEC to propose a novel BH-based computing offloading framework, which is able to allocate limited system resources according to the demands of users in different areas. The strong coupling among computing offloading, BHTP design, and computation resource allocation makes it difficult to design a joint optimization strategy. To minimize the task processing latency, this paper investigates the joint beam hopping and computing offloading design in LEO satellite networks. Using deep   learning   (DL)   algorithms   for   resource   scheduling   is a common approach. However, multi-dimensional resource scheduling design entails high training overhead and poor convergence. Especially as network scale increases, the state and action spaces grow rapidly, making it more challenging to achieve satisfactory performance. Although polling or greedy approaches (e.g., [16] and [17]) have low complexity, they fail to jointly schedule resources in accordance with the optimiza- tion objective, leading to poor latency satisfaction. A summary of the main contributions is provided below. 1) Existing works on LEO SMEC networks often rely on fixed beam illumination strategies, which ignore the diversity of traffic distribution and consequently limit system resource utilization and flexibility. To address this issue, this paper integrates BH technology with computing offloading, and proposes a novel joint BH- computing offloading framework, which can allocate resources based on traffic distribution, and thus increase system resource utilization and flexibility. 2) To minimize the maximum latency of ground users, this paper formulates a joint beam hopping and computing offloading optimization problem involving computing offloading, periodic BHTP design and computation re- source allocation. The resulting problem is a non-linear and non-convex mixed integer problem, where the cou- pling of variables further increases the complexity. 3) To   efficiently   address   the   joint   BH   and   computing offloading problem, we decompose it into two sub- problems: BHTP design, and joint computing offload- ing and computation resource allocation. Specifically, with   a   given   BHTP,   the   joint   computing   offloading and   computation   resource   allocation   sub-problem   is first analyzed and solved. Through relaxation, the sub- problem is decomposed into computing offloading and resource allocation design, enabling the acquisition of the solution via solving the lower-level convex sub- problems iteratively. And then, we can optimize the solutions for the original problem via iterative updates between the two sub-problems. The effectiveness of the proposed joint beam hopping and computing offloading algorithm is validated through simulation results, which show clear advantages over baseline algorithms. Lyu et al. Shuai et al. [18] addressed the dynamic channel state caused by terminal mobility, and proposed a computing offloading approach aimed at enhancing ground users’ satis- faction. To reduce overall latency experienced by terrestrial users,   Qin   et   al.   [19]   proposed   an   offloading   framework that combines centralized training with distributed computing. Zhou et al. [7] designed a multi-agent offloading approach that jointly accounts for queue latency and dynamic channel conditions. To achieve a trade- off between task delay and energy consumption, a computing offloading scheme with dynamic user association is designed in [26]. Hao et al. [20] designed a low-complexity heuristics based offloading scheme with heterogeneous resource allo- cation, which minimizes the total latency subject to caching resource constraints. Zhang et al. [27] introduced a terminal- satellite-cloud three-tier edge computing architecture, and pro- posed a deep learning (DL) based offloading scheme with heterogeneous resource allocation. Tun et al. [28] and Zhang et al. [29] proposed a block coordinate descent (BCD) based computing offloading algorithm. Based on the BCD principle and deep Q learning algorithm, Yang et al. [30] proposed a computing offloading algorithm for SMEC, which maximizes long-term performance. Cui et al. [21] proposed an offloading framework, which jointly accounts for the processing capa- bilities of LEO satellites and the relay functionality of GEO satellites. Cheng et al. [31] accounted for the time-varying available   energy   on   satellites,   and   proposed   a   Lyapunov- based offloading method incorporating user association and heterogeneous resource allocation. However, above studies do not consider multi-beam LEO SMEC scenarios with diverse task distributions. In such cases, satellite beams fail to match user demands dynamically, thus reducing the utilization efficiency of system resources.  B. SYSTEM MODEL AND PROBLEM FORMULATION Fig. 1 illustrates the considered scenario, where the LEO satellite system comprises a total of   N S   satellites covering  N I   ground cells totally. For LEO satellite networks, a satellite always has four inter-satellite links (ISL) with its adjacent satellites, including two intra-plane ISLs between neighboring satellite on the same orbit and two inter-plane ISLs between neighboring satellite on two adjacent orbits [27]. Upon collect- ing the demands of service users, LEO satellites interact and aggregate the data through inter-satellite links, subsequently transmitting   it   to   the   ground   gateway.   The   gateway   then generates resource allocation strategies based on the current system information, and informs the individual nodes with these decisions. Because of the short duration of resource allocation,   typically   lasting   several   beam   hopping   periods (e.g., hundreds of milliseconds) [35], the channel conditions and user associations remain approximately constant during resource allocation. Therefore, the system is considered quasi- static during resource allocation, namely, both the network topology and associated users of LEO satellites remain in- variant during resource allocation [27], [36]. Based on this assumption, we consider that the BH strategy is designed based on periodic BHTP, while the access satellite assignment and onboard computation resources are pre-reserved by the gateway and remain unchanged until all tasks are completed. In   the   LEO   satellite   network   considered   in   this   paper, the set of satellites is represented as   S   =   { 1 ,   2 , ..., N S   } . Employing full frequency reuse technology, each beam is capable of fully utilizing the system’s available bandwidth   B . The set of ground cells is represented as   I   =   { 1 ,   2 , ..., N I   } . In this paper, we assume that there is only one user per ground cell, which accounts for the aggregation of demands across the cell   1   [37], [38]. Consequently, both satellites and users are equipped with computing servers dedicated for task processing. Considering that any ground cell may be covered by multiple satellites, each user needs to select an access satellite to establish a connection. VI. CONCLUSION This paper investigates the joint beam hopping and com- puting offloading problem in LEO satellite networks. To cope with the uneven distribution of user tasks, the beam hopping technology is introduced to enhance flexibility and resource utilization. Targeting the minimization of the maximum total latency, we investigate the joint strategy of computing offload- ing, BHTP design and computation resource allocation. To solve the mixed integer programming problem effectively, we decompose the original problem into multiple sub-problems, and propose a BCD and matching theory based joint beam hopping and computing offloading algorithm. The effective- ness   of   the   proposed   JBHCO-MB   algorithm   is   validated through simulation, showing clear advantages over baseline algorithms.   In   future   work,   we   will   investigate   the   joint beam hopping and computing offloading problem in integrated space-air-ground networks with cooperative computing across different nodes, and extend the system model to scenarios with multiple users within a single ground cell. The future work will focus on designing low-complexity algorithms that not only ensure system efficiency but also satisfy the heterogeneous service demands of users across different regions.
